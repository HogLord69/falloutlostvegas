Fallout: Lost Vegas

Fallout: New Vegas, rebuilt in Fallout 2.

Watch the opening Meet the cast

In development. Goodsprings comes first.

New Vegas's places, people and lines, rebuilt as a classic isometric Fallout.

Lost Vegas takes Fallout: New Vegas apart and rebuilds it inside Fallout 2. The same towns and the same people saying the same lines, seen from above on a hex grid, with turn-based fights and dialogue trees.

Nothing is redrawn by hand. The buildings, rocks and people are New Vegas's own models, rendered through Fallout's camera and cut into the pieces the old engine uses.

It starts where New Vegas starts: on your knees in Goodsprings Cemetery, in front of a man in a checkered suit.

How it's made

A set of tools does the conversion. Every place goes through them in this order.

  1. Read the place

    New Vegas's own data file says where every wall, rock, door and person stands. The converter reads it.

  2. Pull the models

    The meshes and textures for that place come out of the game's archives.

  3. Render through Fallout's camera

    Blender draws each object from Fallout 2's fixed angle. One New Vegas unit is half a Fallout pixel, so a person comes out 58 pixels tall.

  4. Cut into pieces

    Each render is cut into what the old engine understands: scenery, wall columns 32 pixels wide, and floor tiles.

  5. Build the map

    The pieces go down hex by hex where New Vegas had them, and the doors are wired to lead inside.

  6. Add the people

    Each character's New Vegas body is rigged and rendered into 126 animations facing six ways, then given their lines from the game.
